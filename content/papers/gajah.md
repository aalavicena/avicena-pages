##### Abstract

Media narratives shape public tolerance for wildlife and conservation policy legitimacy. Traditional approaches to analyzing conservation discourse are time- and labor-intensive, limiting comparative and large-scale study. We combined the GDELT news database, automated sentiment analysis (VADER), and LLM-based content coding (Deepseek-R1) to analyze English-language human-elephant conflict (HEC) coverage across 13 Asian range states (2016–2024). Among 590 articles, human casualty reports carried significantly more negative sentiment than other incident types (Kruskal-Wallis χ² = 93.9, p < 0.001). Content analysis revealed elephants were predominantly framed as threats, though Indonesia diverged by emphasizing habitat loss. National reporting styles differed markedly — India's coverage was emotionally intense, while China's stressed techno-managerial solutions. This scalable framework demonstrates LLM-assisted media analysis as a practical tool for comparative conservation discourse research.
